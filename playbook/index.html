Get found — the new distribution

Search is dying. Be where the questions actually get asked.

Organic search is collapsing for this category. The moves that replace it are owned and assistant-native, not SEO.

01Be the tool the assistant calls.

The new front door is someone asking an AI. Ship Tellera as an MCP server / assistant integration so that when a user asks ChatGPT or Claude "who owns this house" or "is this VIN salvage," Tellera is the tool it invokes. Treat it as a co-equal front door to the website — it's the single best distribution hedge in the portfolio.

DistributionMCP
02Build the owned channels from day one.

Search won't reliably send traffic and can't be counted on to bring anyone back. A mobile app, push, and email are the durable, LTV-owned channels — and they're what turn a one-time answer into an ongoing relationship. Stand them up early rather than bolting them on.

The wedge & the trust layer

Win on a cleaner promise than the depth players.

How Tellera answers is the differentiator — and the thing that keeps it out of legal and reputational trouble.

03Sell "an answer, not a report."

The depth players sell 40-page narrative dumps. Tellera sells the one synthesized answer to the exact question you asked. That's a cleaner pitch, a faster funnel, and — because you surface less raw data — a smaller compliance and exposure surface. Positioning and de-risking point the same way.

PositioningCompliance-safe
04Keep provenance real under the hood — quiet on the surface.

Over-promising "sources" in marketing is a trap, but an AI answer that's wrong about a real person is a genuine liability. Build citations and honest confidence labels robustly in the data layer, available on demand — while keeping the marketing surface clean. It's not a contradiction; it's the reconciliation.

TrustCompliance
Convert — acquisition & the funnel

From the click to the paywall.

The highest-leverage tests live at the two ends of the funnel: the ad that earns the click and the moment we ask for the card.

05Match the ad to the question, and the question to the page.

The strongest creative poses the exact real-world question — "Who owns the house behind me?", "Is this used car a flood car?" — and maps each ad to one agent and a landing page pre-filled with that question. Scent-matched click → landing → answer preview is the biggest CVR lever, and AI creative can generate question variants cheaply to find winners.

Start hereAcquisition
06Let people ask before they hit the wall.

The typable Deep Search box is the hook. Letting someone type a real question and see a compliant, PII-removed partial answer delivers the "whoa, it works" moment before you ask for anything — lowering bounce and raising intent at the paywall.

AcquisitionActivation
07The blurred answer is the conversion moment. Tune the reveal.

Conversion hinges on how much of the answer you show: enough synthesized result to be credible, the payoff blurred. A/B the reveal amount — show the address, blur the owner? show a confidence badge and one field? This single test likely moves signup-attempt rate more than anything else in the funnel.

Keep them — the compounding loop

Retention comes from ongoing value, not cancel friction.

A one-time lookup has no reason to renew. These two turn Tellera into something that gets more valuable the longer you stay.

08Turn every answer into a standing watch.

A one-time answer gives no reason to keep paying; a living watchlist does. Convert answers into re-running monitors — "alert me if this property sells, a new court record appears, this VIN's title changes." This is both the retention engine and a premium tier ("Tellera Monitoring"), and it's the post-paywall move with the most leverage.

Start hereRetentionRevenue
09Make the answer history a compounding asset.

Every question builds a personal, re-runnable knowledge base. The longer someone stays, the more they'd lose by leaving — accumulated value is retention you get for free. Gate history depth and export by tier so it also drives upgrades.

Grow the account — ARPU

Make each subscriber worth more.

Tellera's breadth and household nature are levers competitors can't easily copy.

10Use Tellera as the front door to the whole LTV portfolio.

When a property question comes up, upsell Ownerly / NeighborWho depth; a VIN routes to full Bumper history; an ancestry thread into FamFinder. Tellera monetizes LTV's breadth as one product — incremental revenue from existing subscribers at zero new acquisition cost.

ExpansionARPU
11Offer household and multi-seat plans.

Property and genealogy are household-natural — one account, several family members. A family tier is a higher price point and stickier (more people invested, harder to cancel), and it's the natural on-ramp to the B2B multi-seat model.

The platform business

Where the margin compounds.

The consumer product proves the surface; the same agents become a durable B2B revenue engine.

12Stand up the permissible-purpose B2B / API / MCP tier.

This is the durable, high-margin revenue the whole strategy points toward. Businesses pay far more per seat or call than consumers — affluent-homeowner-by-location, business lookup, vehicle evaluation — all reusing the agents you've already built, gated for compliance. Sequence it after the consumer motion is proven, but design the agents from the start so the same surface can serve it.

Rigor — know it works, know it pays

Measured, not vibed.

Two disciplines that make everything above safe to scale.

13Know what every answer costs.

AI spend is metered, not free. Track cost-per-answer and cost-per-agent from launch, and feed it straight into ROAS so you only scale paid traffic where the unit economics actually work. It's what lets spend grow responsibly instead of buying unprofitable volume.

EconomicsUnit cost
14Measure answer quality — don't vibe it.

Accuracy on real records is the whole game: a wrong answer is both a liability and a trust/churn killer. Build an eval suite so quality is measured as you add agents — and so accuracy becomes marketing proof rather than a claim.
